### What Buyers Usually Review

Buyers often compare visual appearance, moisture stability, odor during use, ash behavior, and packaging condition. They also ask whether you can keep the same specification from one order to the next.

| Evaluation Area | What to Check | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Raw material | Shell source, cleanliness, and traceability | Supports consistent output |
| Odor | Neutral smell during storage and use | Improves end-user experience |
| Ash color | Light, natural ash after burning | Indicates cleaner combustion |
| Packaging | Sealed, intact, and labeled cartons or bags | Reduces damage in transit |
| Export readiness | Documents and shipping coordination | Helps buyers plan inventory |

## Raw Material Selection

You start with coconut shell material that is clean, dry, and suitable for briquette production. The goal is to reduce unwanted impurities before carbonization and shaping begin.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Source the shells" icon="download">
    Choose coconut shell feedstock from reliable suppliers and inspect it for dirt, sand, and mixed debris.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sort and clean" icon="settings">
    Remove foreign material so the feedstock is easier to process and more consistent in the furnace.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Carbonize and cool" icon="zap">
    Process the shells under controlled conditions, then cool the carbon completely before grinding.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Grind and screen" icon="check-circle">
    Mill the char into a consistent particle size and screen out oversized fragments.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="tip">

If you document the source region, moisture condition, and screening method for each batch, you make buyer review easier and reduce follow-up questions.

</Callout>

## Quality Control Checks

Quality control helps you keep each batch close to the same specification. A simple checklist is often enough for a starter documentation set, as long as you describe what you inspect and when you inspect it.

### Suggested Review Points

You can structure inspection around production checkpoints:

1. Incoming raw material inspection  
2. Pre-carbonization sorting  
3. Post-carbonization cooling and grinding  
4. Pressing and shaping review  
5. Final packing and shipment release  

## Product Characteristics

Clear product characteristics help buyers understand what they are evaluating. For this category, the most common points are odor, ash color, shape consistency, and burn behavior.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Odor" icon="help-circle">
    The finished briquettes should have a neutral or minimal odor profile during storage and use. You should document any aroma that appears after ignition so buyers know what to expect.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Ash Color" icon="check">
    Buyers often prefer a natural, light ash appearance after combustion. You can describe the expected ash tone in your product sheet and include photos when available.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Shape" icon="package">
    Consistent shape makes packing and handling easier. You should note whether the product uses pillow, hexagonal, or custom forms, then keep the description aligned with actual output.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Burn Performance" icon="fire">
    Stable heat output and predictable burn time are useful specification points. Keep your documentation descriptive and avoid unsupported numerical claims unless you can verify them.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Practical Buyer Checklist

Before you approve a purchase order, make sure your documentation answers these questions:

- Is the raw material source defined?
- Does the batch follow the same quality control steps every time?
- Are the briquettes presented with a natural appearance and low odor?
- Is the ash color described clearly enough for buyer review?
- Do the packaging and shipment notes support export handling?
